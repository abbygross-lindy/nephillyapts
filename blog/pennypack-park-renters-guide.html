A Renter's Guide to Pennypack Park

April 7, 2026
Northeast Philly Apts › Blog › Things To Do

Pennypack Park is one of Northeast Philadelphia's best-kept secrets—though if you're renting in the area, it won't stay secret for long. With 1,600 acres of trails, creeks, and green space, it's the kind of amenity that makes NE Philly living worth it.

What Makes Pennypack Park Special

Unlike smaller neighborhood parks, Pennypack is a legitimate nature preserve that runs for miles through Northeast Philadelphia. You can walk, run, bike, or just sit by the creek without feeling like you're squeezed into a postage stamp of green space.

The park follows Pennypack Creek, and the trails are well-maintained and accessible year-round. Whether you're into serious hiking or just want a place to walk your dog, the park has options.

Best Trails and Access Points

The main trail runs about nine miles — paved through the Philadelphia stretch, crushed stone as it continues north into Montgomery County. It's wide, flat, and great for jogging, biking, or walking, and there are unpaved side trails if you want a more natural experience.

For renters in Rhawnhurst, the easiest access points are off Verree Road and Pine Road. If you're at Longwood Manor, you're literally minutes from the park. From the Far Northeast communities, you can access the park via Byberry Road or Bustleton Avenue.

Activities and Amenities

Pennypack Park isn't just trails. There are picnic areas, playgrounds, sports fields, and even fishing spots along the creek. In warmer months, it's common to see families grilling, kids playing, and groups using the fields for pickup games.

The park is also dog-friendly, which is a big deal for renters with pets. It's one of the few places in the city where your dog can actually run and explore (on-leash, of course).

Year-Round Use

One of the best things about Pennypack is that it's not a summer-only park. The trails are maintained in winter, and you'll see people out there year-round. Fall is especially nice when the leaves change, and even winter walks along the creek can be surprisingly peaceful.

Why It Matters for Renters

Having 1,600 acres of green space minutes from your apartment is a quality-of-life upgrade that's hard to overstate. If you're used to neighborhoods where "park access" means a small playground or a patch of grass, Pennypack will feel like a different world.

For renters considering Northeast Philadelphia, proximity to Pennypack Park should be high on your list. It's one of those amenities you use more than you think you will—and once you have it, you don't want to give it up.
